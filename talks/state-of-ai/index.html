Skills & Capability Governance Workstream 2

Skills are sticky notes in the wild

  • Teams and individuals create skills locally — fast, useful, ungoverned
  • No inventory: nobody knows how many skills exist, what they do, or who owns them
  • No evaluation: skills are used but never measured for quality, cost, or drift
  • No reuse tracking: the same capability is reinvented across teams
  • Skills become shadow IT capabilities — powerful but invisible to the organization
Without evaluation, there is no long-term benefit. Skills that are never measured cannot improve, compound, or be trusted at scale.

The governance gap

Invisible proliferation — skills multiply without central visibility, creating ungoverned enterprise IT capabilities
No quality signal — without evaluation, bad skills survive as long as good ones
No ownership — when the creator leaves, the skill becomes orphaned but still runs
No cost attribution — token spend is invisible and unmanaged per skill

From unmanaged skill usage → governed skills library

Say: "Skills are the new shadow IT. Teams create them because they are useful, but without inventory, evaluation, and ownership, they become ungoverned enterprise capabilities. The next architecture problem is turning skills from local productivity boosters into evaluated, reusable assets. Without evaluation, there is no compounding benefit — just growing risk."

The missing feedback loop

Most projects have no systematic process to create, review, and improve specs. Specifications are written once and never revisited. This is where the senior hero pattern comes from — only experienced architects intuitively close this loop.

Every generated artifact must carry receipts: what evidence was consumed, what model produced it, what gate accepted it, and what feedback was captured.

Say: "Traceability and determinism in AI-supported SDLC are not solved properly in the industry. But the deeper problem is the missing spec feedback loop. Most projects write specs once and move on. The senior hero pattern exists precisely because experienced architects close this loop intuitively — they revise specs based on what they learn during execution. Making this loop explicit and systematic is how we replace heroics with a repeatable process."

Semantic Layer & Evidence Graph Workstream 4

The semantic layer is not a data lake, not GraphRAG, and not a BI semantic model. It is the governed context substrate for AI.

Example: an SDLC evidence graph captures

Code facts & business rules
Requirements & specifications
Tickets, SOPs & operational evidence
SME feedback & human corrections
Agent traces & tool outputs
Validation & review decisions

This is one domain graph. The same pattern applies to sales, operations, compliance, and any governed AI workflow.

Why build the semantic layer

Quality: Precise context produces better artifacts. Less hallucination, more grounded outputs, easier to validate.
Traceability: Every AI output links back to evidence. Auditable, explainable, governable.
Cost reduction: Smaller, specific prompts mean fewer tokens. Route to cheaper models when context is strong. Cache reusable context.
Compounding value: Every correction, review, and feedback enriches the graph. AI gets better over time, not just faster.
Say: "The semantic layer is both the quality layer and the cost layer. If context is precise, prompts are smaller, retrieval is better, generated artifacts are easier to validate, and we can route more work to smaller or cheaper models. The SDLC graph is just one example — the same pattern applies to any domain where AI needs governed, traceable context."

Roadmap: visibility → repeatability → scale → autonomy

Workstream 6 months 1 year 2 years Future
1. Agentic Platform Inventory skills, tools, MCPs, agents. Introduce Cards. Measure governed vs ungoverned. BU-level MCP registries, evaluation, observability, HITL, ownership, cost tracking. Enterprise control plane: lifecycle, policy, model routing, telemetry, auditability, cost. Goal-oriented agents inside governed semantic boundaries.
2. Skills & Capabilities Baseline work profiles. Seed skills library from hackathon artifacts. Reusable evaluated skills, ownership, regression checks, coding-agent governance. L3 guided execution: agents generate PRs, specs, tests; humans approve risky steps. Bounded L4 / AI-native delivery for safe domains.
3. Brownfield & Modernization Select 1–2 pilots. Build first KB from code, DBs, tickets, SOPs, SME validation. Run source → KB → spec → artifact → validation → review loop. Prove repeatability. Modernization factory: reusable agents, evidence graph, sequencing, portfolio metrics. Debt retirement as portfolio capability, not heroic work.
4. Semantic Layer Define evidence model. Connect initial tool graphs. Capture feedback & traces. Use for retrieval, prompt grounding, spec generation, validation, traceability. Introduce encoders. Shared enterprise AI context substrate with provenance, drift, policy-aware retrieval. Business-realtime semantic layer. Agents query & write through governed paths.
5. AI FinOps Make cost visible. Track cost per work profile. Spend limits, model tiering, prompt optimization, caching, cost per artifact. Portfolio economics: per accepted PR, migrated module, retired vulnerability. Frontier models as scarce resources; encoders carry the volume.
6. Adoption Productize Cowork for managers. Teach delegation, review, governance literacy. Role-specific playbooks for BAs, architects, QA, product, operations. AI-enabled operating model: leaders supervise via KPIs, cards, traces, gates. Digital workforce supervision: humans maintain intent & policies.
Say: "The mistake would be to jump from successful workshops to broad AI rollout. The right sequence is visibility, then governed repeatability, then industrial scale, and only then bounded autonomy."

What we actually see in the market

Technology push, not business pull

Most companies are head over heels scrambling to build AI platforms. We see a common lack of business pull — teams build because they can, not because a business outcome demands it.

AI cost is severely underappreciated

Workshop costs feel invisible today. Once adoption scales beyond engineering, unrestricted frontier-model access will hit budget walls nobody is planning for.

No semantic layer understanding

Very few organizations recognize the semantic layer as the strategic asset it is. Most conflate it with data lakes or RAG — missing the governance, traceability, and cost benefits.

This will change fast

OpenAI Frontier and Claude Cowork / Claude Code are making agentic AI accessible to non-engineers. When managers and business users adopt AI co-working, the demand for governance, cost control, and semantic context will become urgent — not optional.

The question is not whether your organization will need governed AI. It is whether you will be ready when adoption outpaces your operating model.
Say: "Right now most companies are building AI platforms because they feel they should, not because a business outcome pulled them there. Cost is invisible, the semantic layer is unknown, and governance is an afterthought. But OpenAI Frontier and Claude Cowork are about to put agentic AI into the hands of managers and business users. When that happens, the demand for everything we discussed today — governance, cost control, semantic context — will become urgent overnight."
